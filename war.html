Chapter V · The war chapter

Rifleman,
9th Infantry

William Cecil Giles of Knifley, Kentucky. Inducted October 1943. In France by the summer of 1944. Wounded on the third of August. Purple Heart.

KNIFLEY SOLDIER GIVEN HONORABLE DISCHARGEAdair County News · 6 June 1945

He was nineteen, a young farmer from Knifley. (His discharge would later sketch him in a line: blue eyes, brown hair, five feet eleven, 138 pounds.) On 5 October 1943 the Army inducted ; on the 26th he entered active service at Louisville. A few weeks later the Adair County News printed the list of local men sent from the reception center at Fort Thomas to Camp Atterbury, Indiana — and there he was: “William C. Giles, Knifley.”

He trained as a rifleman — military occupational specialty 745 — and qualified as a marksman with the M1 rifle. On 1 July 1944 he sailed from the United States. On 16 July he arrived in the European Theater of Operations. His outfit: Company E, 9th Infantry.

Eighteen days later, on 3 August 1944, he was wounded in France. The home paper put it plainly: “severe leg wounds.”

        Afterward

        The long way home

        William left the European Theater on 26 December 1944 and was back in the United States on 3 January 1945. That spring he married . On 30 May 1945, at William Beaumont General Hospital in El Paso, Texas, the Army gave him his honorable discharge — a Certificate of Disability for Discharge, with the remark that he was not physically qualified for re-enlistment. He wore the European–African–Middle Eastern Theater ribbon with one battle star, and the Purple Heart.

        He reached Adair County on Saturday, 1 June 1945. The paper itself is stamped “filed for record” on 18 June 1945, and it carries the county clerk’s certification, dated 15 August 1960, that it is a true copy from Discharge Book No. 2. That certified copy stayed in the family. William’s daughter Alice kept it and later gave it to Joshua.

        His regiment fought on without him: the siege of Brest, the Battle of the Bulge, and the end of the war near Pilsen, Czechoslovakia.
